### Legal-review items

- Chargebacks finalized after month 12 from a timely audit: Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25) allows a chargeback only "as a result of a timely conducted audit" and then requires a meeting within 30 days after the audit, a dealer response period of at least 45 days, completion of internal dispute processes, a final written notice and a 30-day protest window, a sequence that will routinely end more than 12 months after the claim or incentive was paid. The same subsection says that after the 12-month periods have elapsed all such payments "shall be deemed final and incontrovertible for any reason notwithstanding any otherwise applicable law, and the motor vehicle dealer shall not be subject to any chargeback or repayment." The statute does not say whether a timely audit preserves a chargeback taken after month 12, and nothing in §§ 320.60-320.70 reconciles the two sentences. ACTION: Obtain legal interpretation before taking any chargeback more than 12 months after the payment date, and schedule Florida audits early enough in the window that the meeting, 45-day response and final notice can be completed inside 12 months of payment. [Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25)]
    quote: "as a result of a timely conducted audit, impose a chargeback against a motor vehicle dealer for warranty, maintenance, or other service-related payments or incentive payments" (Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25))
    quote: "shall be deemed final and incontrovertible for any reason notwithstanding any otherwise applicable law, and the motor vehicle dealer shall not be subject to any chargeback or repayment." (Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25))
- Calendar-year audit cap and incentive audits: Fla. Stat. § 320.696(2) makes it prohibited adverse action to conduct or threaten "any warranty, retail customer repair, or other service-related audit more frequently than once each calendar year." Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25) regulates audits of "warranty, maintenance, and other service-related payments or incentive payments" as parallel categories with separate 12-month windows, but the calendar-year cap does not name incentive audits, and no provision says whether an incentive audit is an "other service-related audit" or whether a separate incentive audit in the same calendar year as a warranty audit is permitted. The statute does not reconcile the two. ACTION: Obtain legal interpretation before conducting a second audit of any kind at a Florida dealer in the same calendar year; until then treat incentive audits as subject to the once-per-calendar-year cap and to the "reasonable and periodic" standard. [Fla. Stat. § 320.696(2); § 320.64(25)]
    quote: "conducting or threatening to conduct any warranty, retail customer repair, or other service-related audit more frequently than once each calendar year" (Fla. Stat. § 320.696(2))
    quote: "Audits of incentive payments shall be performed only during the 12-month period immediately following the date the incentive was paid." (Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25))
- Service contracts issued by a non-manufacturer common entity: Fla. Stat. § 320.696(1)(a) requires compensation for work under a warranty, maintenance plan, extended warranty, certified pre-owned warranty or service contract "issued by the licensee or its common entity, unless issued by a common entity that is not a manufacturer," which carves such contracts out of the compensation rules. Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25) limits audits of "warranty, maintenance, and other service-related payments" to 12 months from payment and imposes the chargeback process without that carve-out, while also describing the audit as one "to determine the validity of paid claims as provided in s. 320.696." The statute does not say whether the (25) window and process apply to audits of service-contract claims that fall outside § 320.696(1)(a). ACTION: Obtain legal interpretation before auditing or charging back service-contract claims paid by or for a non-manufacturer affiliate on any basis that would not satisfy § 320.64(25); until then apply the (25) window, grounds and process to all service-related payments. [Fla. Stat. § 320.696(1)(a); § 320.64(25)]
    quote: "under a warranty or maintenance plan, extended warranty, certified pre-owned warranty, or a service contract, issued by the licensee or its common entity, unless issued by a common entity that is not a manufacturer" (Fla. Stat. § 320.696(1)(a))
    quote: "Audits of warranty, maintenance, and other service-related payments shall be performed by an applicant or licensee only during the 12-month period immediately following the date the claim was paid." (Fla. Stat. § 320.64(25))

### Legal-review items

- Cure without rebuttal (warranty chargebacks): § 3065(e)(3) bars a chargeback when the franchisee "rebuts any disapproval and cures any material noncompliance" before the applicable deadline. § 3065.1(g)(5), for incentive claims, separately provides that if the franchisee "cures any material noncompliance relating to a claim, the franchisor shall not chargeback the dealer for that claim"; § 3065(e)(5) contains no such sentence. The statute does not say whether a warranty claim that is cured but not otherwise rebutted is protected from chargeback. ACTION: Treat a timely cure of a documentation-only disapproval as a bar to chargeback; obtain legal interpretation before charging back a cured warranty claim on any other ground. [Cal. Veh. Code § 3065(e)(3), (e)(5); § 3065.1(g)(5)]
    quote: "If the franchisee rebuts any disapproval and cures any material noncompliance relating to a claim before the applicable deadline, the franchisor shall not chargeback the franchisee for that claim." (Cal. Veh. Code § 3065(e)(3))
    quote: "If the franchisee cures any material noncompliance relating to a claim, the franchisor shall not chargeback the dealer for that claim." (Cal. Veh. Code § 3065.1(g)(5))
- Protest waivers in warranty-audit settlements: § 11713.3(g)(1)(A)-(B) and (g)(2) make void any agreement, release or waiver that modifies a franchisor duty under Division 2 Chapter 6 or limits a dealer's right to file or pursue a Board protest. The only audit-settlement carve-out, (g)(3)(E), covers a waiver of a § 3065.1 incentive-claim protest given after disapproval; (g)(3)(A) protects settlement terms only once a protest, civil action or other proceeding exists. The statute does not say whether a pre-protest settlement of warranty (§ 3065) audit findings that waives or limits protest rights is enforceable. ACTION: Obtain legal review of any audit settlement, acknowledgment or repayment agreement that waives or limits a dealer's § 3065 protest rights before a protest has been filed. [Cal. Veh. Code § 11713.3(g)(1)(A)-(B), (g)(2), (g)(3)(A), (g)(3)(E)]
    quote: "Limits or constrains the right of a dealer to file, pursue, or submit evidence in connection with a protest before the board." (Cal. Veh. Code § 11713.3(g)(1)(B))
    quote: "Prohibit a dealer from waiving its right to file a protest pursuant to Section 3065.1 if the waiver agreement is entered into after a franchisor incentive program claim has been disapproved by the franchisor" (Cal. Veh. Code § 11713.3(g)(3)(E))
- Part return on audit chargebacks: § 3065(c) requires a franchisor that disallows a claim for a defective part on the ground that the part is not defective to return the part at its own expense or reimburse the dealer's cost, at the franchisor's option. § 3065(e), which governs chargebacks of previously approved claims, does not cross-reference (c). The statute does not say whether (c) applies when a paid parts claim is charged back after audit on a not-defective finding. ACTION: Before charging back a paid parts claim on a "part not defective" finding, obtain legal interpretation of whether § 3065(c) requires return of the part or reimbursement of the dealer's part cost. [Cal. Veh. Code § 3065(c), (e)(2)]
    quote: "If any franchisor disallows a franchisee’s claim for a defective part, alleging that the part, in fact, is not defective, the franchisor shall return the part alleged not to be defective to the franchisee at the expense of the franchisor" (Cal. Veh. Code § 3065(c))
