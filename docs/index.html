RESEARCH NOTES

What this reference covers

This reference compares public warranty reimbursement provisions for franchised passenger-vehicle dealers in all 50 states. Initial research: September 21, 2026. Selected corrections and v3 import: September 25, 2026.

Labor by coverage

The view contains all 200 state / coverage classifications from Claude’s September 25, 2026 v3 research, imported September 25. Source types are 40 official-statute records, 5 enacted-law records and 5 code reproductions. Each coverage entry retains its quotation, pinpoint cite, confidence, notes and conditions. These are supplied research classifications, not a claim that all sources were independently rechecked during import.

The date selector changes classifications and totals. Through September 30, Rhode Island factory hours are Statute silent on time and CPO is Not addressed. From October 1, factory hours are Actual technician time and CPO is Required. The published v3 tally matches the October 1 view. Required describes coverage scope, not an unconditional reimbursement method. Not addressed means the reviewed statute is silent, not that no law applies. Factory time is a broad time-allowance category. Mississippi’s qualified-technician benchmark is Factory time, not individual clock time. NC and PA service-contract rate and hours are not established. Wisconsin’s adjustment sits in the hourly-rate formula; OEM hours remain the paid-hours basis and no second multiplier applies.

Rules, rates and payments

State law generally supplies a reimbursement method. Actual payments depend on retailer-specific terms, applicable methods and dates. Internal retailer payment data were not supplied.

How the checks work

A checked box means the defined feature was identified in the reviewed provision. It can represent a required method, an available election or a conditional fallback. An empty box means the feature was not identified under that definition, not a legal finding that no such rule exists.

The two common retail benchmarks appear for all states. In states such as Missouri and Kentucky they include broader reasonable-compensation references. The two shared retail benchmarks are hidden by default to focus on differences. Uncheck Hide shared retail benchmarks to show them.

Source links and verification are separate

Each state records three different facts: where the original text can be accessed, whether the link was actually tested, and which legal statements were checked. Official access pages may require navigation. An enacted-law PDF and a code edition have different dates; neither automatically proves that later amendments were checked.

Official text reviewed preserves the earlier official-source review. Partial official check identifies specific provisions checked during this update; other statements may still depend on a reproduction. Reproduction-based research identifies the third-party source used. Original research dates and new evidence records are retained separately.

Question marks in the newer columns mean classification is pending. Empty boxes mean a feature was not identified under its definition. Both are clickable. The two approval concepts are kept separate: setting a reimbursement rate and paying an individual claim.

Terms that affect the dollars

Parts markup
Profit amount divided by parts cost. A $100 part reimbursed at $160 has a 60% markup.
Gross margin
Profit amount divided by reimbursement. The same $100 part at $160 has a 37.5% gross margin.
Posted vs. effective labor rate
Posted is the advertised rate. Effective is labor revenue divided by the applicable hours in the qualifying sample. The required denominator matters.
Hourly rate vs. paid hours
Labor payment is hourly rate × allowed hours. A time multiplier changes the hours; Wisconsin’s method incorporates the time difference into the rate.
Sampling period vs. recency limit
A sampling period describes how much activity to submit. A recency limit describes how old that evidence can be.

Dollar examples above are illustrative only.

Scope of the grouping

The 20 features summarize core payment methods and sample-selection procedures found in the research. They are not an exhaustive inventory of every warranty-law requirement. Claim deadlines, rate-change frequency, additional dispute procedures, detailed exclusions, free-part treatment and special assemblies remain in the underlying provisions and state notes.

RV and heavy-truck provisions, independent repair-shop pricing and retail customer estimates remain outside the comparison. Service-contract and CPO reimbursement are classified separately in the v3 labor view.
