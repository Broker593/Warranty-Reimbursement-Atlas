Quick overview

A 50-state quick reference to dealer warranty-reimbursement law: labor rates, paid hours, parts, claims and chargebacks, and whether service contracts and CPO are covered. Built from public statutes.

What's here

  • State Rules (start here) — one row per state: labor rate, rate increase requests, manufacturer response, paid hours, parts markup, and service contracts and CPO. Click a state for its key facts, with the statute quotes and sources one click deeper.
  • News — law changes and rulings (up to 5 a week) plus industry commentary from reliable trade and legal publications (up to 3 a week), labeled separately, with the list of sources checked.
  • Downloads — the Excel workbook (everything on the site), one-page state PDFs and one research data file.
  • Dashboard Overview — rule counts across all 50 states: how many states give each answer, with a plain-English definition of every answer and the states behind it.
  • Map: U.S. Audit Climate — the audit climate score (0–100) on a U.S. map: how restrictive each state's law is toward warranty audits, chargebacks and rate validation. Hover or click a state for how it was scored.
  • Cases & Laws — key decisions and legislation on rate submissions, audits and chargebacks, each labeled with how it was verified.
  • Update log — the Monday law-change checks and site updates.

How to read it

Yes
The state's warranty rate and time rules apply to that coverage.
Conditional
Applies only if a condition is met, usually who issues or pays for the contract.
Not covered
The statute excludes it or limits the rule to manufacturer-issued work.
Silent · Not set in statute
The statute says nothing. Contracts, regulations or other law may still apply.
Full details
The statute quote, pinpoint cite and conditions behind each key fact.

Keep in mind

  • Internal use only. Research summary, not legal advice. Verify against the cited statute and take disputes to Legal.
  • No SOA rates or dealer payment data.
  • Checked for law changes every Monday. Each state shows Last verified and Law last amended.
  • Distributor-franchised states (New England, New York, northern New Jersey and Hawaii) are flagged. There, the rules apply to the distributor that holds the dealer's franchise.
  • Scope: franchised passenger-vehicle dealers. RV, heavy-truck and independent repair-shop rules are out of scope.

Glossary
Hourly rate vs. paid hours
Labor payment = hourly rate × allowed hours. A time multiplier changes the hours; Wisconsin builds the time difference into the rate.
Posted vs. effective labor rate
Posted is the advertised rate. Effective is labor revenue divided by the hours in the qualifying sample.
Parts markup vs. gross margin
A $100 part reimbursed at $160 is a 60% markup and a 37.5% gross margin.
Sample period vs. age limit
The sample period is how much repair-order history to submit; the age limit is how old it can be.
