RESEARCH NOTES

What this reference covers

This reference compares public warranty reimbursement provisions for franchised passenger-vehicle dealers in all 50 states. It was researched on September 21, 2026.

Rules, rates and payments

State law generally supplies a reimbursement method. An actual SOA payment also depends on the retailer’s approved parts markup, hourly rate, effective dates and applicable labor-time rules. SOA’s retailer rate master and paid claims were not available for this research.

How the checks work

A checked box means the defined feature was identified in the reviewed provision. It can represent a required method, an available election or a conditional fallback. An empty box means the feature was not identified under that definition, not a legal finding that no such rule exists.

The two common retail benchmarks appear for all states. In states such as Missouri and Kentucky they include broader reasonable-compensation references. The two shared retail benchmarks are hidden by default to focus on differences. Uncheck Hide shared retail benchmarks to show them.

Source labels mean what was reviewed

Official statute reviewed: the state’s official statutory text was read. Third-party text reviewed; official verification pending: the research used a reproduction or summary. Naming or linking to a statute does not mean its official text was checked. Original text links appear first wherever located. “Official access page” means navigation or an interactive viewer is required; it does not mean the section text was retrieved. The publisher and website are displayed on every source link.

Source coverage

Official statutory text was checked for 11 states. Most other entries use Armatus statutory reproductions, with their update dates retained. Alaska uses a 2025 Justia code reproduction; New Jersey’s effective date was corroborated by NJ CAR. Some secondary pages date to 2024.

This is a working public-source comparison, not an exhaustive update of all 2026 legislation, regulations and case law. Each state detail links to the evidence used and the original source where located. Original text is linked for 33 states (Mississippi covers the labor provision only); 7 more have official access pages. Original links remain unconfirmed for Alabama, Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, New Mexico and Oklahoma. Finding a link does not complete a legal comparison of the summary.

Terms that affect the dollars

Parts markup
Profit amount divided by parts cost. A $100 part reimbursed at $160 has a 60% markup.
Gross margin
Profit amount divided by reimbursement. The same $100 part at $160 has a 37.5% gross margin.
Posted vs. effective labor rate
Posted is the advertised rate. Effective is labor revenue divided by the applicable hours in the qualifying sample. The required denominator matters.
Hourly rate vs. paid hours
Labor payment is hourly rate × allowed hours. A time multiplier changes the hours; Wisconsin’s method incorporates the time difference into the rate.
Sampling period vs. recency limit
A sampling period describes how much activity to submit. A recency limit describes how old that evidence can be.

Dollar examples above are illustrative only.

Scope of the grouping

The 13 features summarize core payment methods and sample-selection procedures found in the research. They are not an exhaustive inventory of every warranty-law requirement. Claim deadlines, rate-change frequency, dispute standards, detailed exclusions, free-part treatment and special assemblies remain in the underlying provisions and state notes.

RV and heavy-truck provisions, independent repair-shop pricing, retail customer estimates and unrelated service-contract rules are outside the comparison.
